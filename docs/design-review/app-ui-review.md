# App UI final usability review

**Verdict: APPROVE WITH REQUIRED FIXES.**

The monochrome Quiet Instrument direction is approved for implementation. The large live timer, clearly separated transports, restrained sidebar and readable document surfaces give Wffl an identity without making the core workflow harder. Preserve that direction. This is a focused final usability review of the proposal, not a full audit or approval of a shipped native redesign.

Reviewed: handoff README, TODO, DESIGN and PRODUCT; mockup HTML; local captures of Home, Recording, dark Dashboard, dark Summary and dark Settings; relevant native view/state mapping. No browser session was changed. Source is still the cream/sage/serif app. Native interaction, VoiceOver and resizing have not been exercised on a redesigned build.

## Six required fixes

1. **Adapt the composition to the actual window rather than scaling it.** The mockup is fixed at 1240×780 with hidden overflow; the real window permits 900×560 (`WfflApp.swift:117`). Home reserves a 400px readiness column, 56px gap and 128px horizontal padding in addition to its sidebar. That cannot work at the native minimum, and the recording transcript already extends below the capture. Specify a compact layout: stack Home readiness under its CTA, retain transports without scrolling, put long transcript/settings/summary content in independent scroll regions, and wrap summary owner/date columns. Keep body type at readable native sizes. **Mapping:** `HomeView`, `RecordingSurface` in `RecordingBar.swift`, `ContentView`, `SummaryView`, `SettingsPane`, `DashboardView`. Acceptance: all actions remain reachable at 900×560 and 1240×780; long titles and real transcripts do not truncate essential information. This is faithful responsive implementation, not new functionality.

2. **Make privacy and recognition claims reflect actual state.** Settings shows “Network: model downloads only” beside copy admitting opt-in cloud summaries. Home says summaries happen entirely here regardless of configuration. Use “Local processing” only for a genuinely local provider/endpoint; otherwise name the configured destination and state that transcripts are sent there. Keep the audio-stays-local claim distinct. Do not say other voices “get their names after you stop”: postprocessing separates unknown voices as Speaker 1/2, and the user renames them; known voice matches may recover a prior name. Add a quiet, visible rename affordance beside unknown speaker chips. Retain the existing honest speaker-count fallback. **Mapping:** `SettingsPane`, `SettingsView` provider settings, `SidebarView`, `HomeView`, `SummaryView`, `TranscriptView.speakerChip`/rename popover, `MeetingDetailView.speakerLabel`. These are faithful labels for existing behavior. Do not introduce automatic identity claims.

3. **Design paused, muted, saving and postprocessing states before treating the recording-to-summary transition as approved.** The prototype jumps from Stop & save to summary generation; the actual pipeline can be stopping, re-transcribing, attributing voices and cleaning before a summary is available. Keep Stop disabled with “Saving…” while stopping, then show the true processing stage and allow the saved raw transcript to remain accessible. Pause must visibly become Resume, the lamp/state word must say Paused, and mic mute must explicitly say system audio continues. Do not equate absent transcript text with absent recording. **Mapping:** recorder state handling in `RecordingBar.swift`, `ContentView`, `MeetingDetailView` import/progress banner, `TranscriptView`, `SummaryView`. Mute mic already exists and is retained. Preserve current state behavior and surface it clearly; a multi-stage production pipeline is not an instantaneous animation.

4. **Carry the native keyboard model through the visual redesign.** Preserve existing ⌘N and ⌘I menu commands. Render transports, navigation, tabs, model choices, search and rename as native controls with visible focus, descriptive accessibility labels and disabled states. Provide keyboard operation for Pause/Resume, Mute/Unmute and Stop through the existing command architecture; choose documented conflict-free shortcuts rather than implying that the mockup’s displayed hint implements anything. Search must be a real field. Do not make read-only action-item circles look like persistent task controls: `MarkdownView` currently renders Markdown checkboxes as images. Keep them clearly document notation unless persistence is separately scoped. **Mapping:** `WfflApp` commands, `SidebarView`, `RecordingBar`, `MeetingDetailView`, `TranscriptView`, `SummaryView.MarkdownView`. Native keyboard access is faithful usability work; persistent task management would be new behavior.

5. **Resolve typography and contrast as native assets/tokens.** The light mockup uses #9A9A96 for small headings/counts/search text, approximately 2.6:1 on the ground; use a darker text token meeting 4.5:1 for ordinary small text. Do not reuse the decorative grey headline color for functional labels. Keep transcript/summary body around 14–16pt, with comfortable leading; reserve tiny mono for secondary timecodes. Hanken/Fragment currently exist as website WOFF2 files, while `Theme.display` is system serif and `Package.swift` declares no font resources. Bundle licensed desktop-compatible font files, register them via the app’s resource mechanism and supply a deliberate system sans/mono fallback. `scripts/bundle.sh` currently copies only the executable, plist and icon: extend bundle assembly to carry font resources before signing; declaring SwiftPM resources alone is insufficient. **Mapping:** `Theme.swift`, `Package.swift`, app resource registration, document/list view font calls. Verify both themes and Increase Contrast on the built app. This implements the approved typography; packaging is required rather than an optional polish pass.

6. **Implement real adaptive appearance before calling dark mode complete.** `WfflApp.init` forces `.aqua`, and `Theme.swift` contains fixed warm colors and a multi-color speaker palette. Removing the appearance pin alone would expose unreadable native controls. Replace the theme with resolved light/dark semantic colors, migrate hardcoded recording colors and selected/disabled/control foregrounds, then remove the pin. Preserve separation between selected sidebar pill and selected meeting row, and use speaker text identity rather than color dependence. Keep the floating recording bar deliberately dark in both modes if desired, with its own explicit foreground tokens. **Mapping:** `WfflApp.swift`, `Theme.swift`, `ContentView`, `RecordingBar`, settings controls and transcript chips. Acceptance: launch in either system appearance, change appearance while open, and check native popovers, menus, fields and progress states as well as cards. This is the faithful dark counterpart already promised in the design.

## Scope guard

The redesign can reuse existing recording, mute, pause, imports, provider configuration, raw/cleaned transcripts, speaker rename/library, notes and Markdown summaries. Do not silently add persistent action-item completion, automatic assignment/due-date extraction, or guaranteed immediate summaries just because the sample renders them attractively. Engine names, sizes and recommendation labels must come from supported installed/downloadable model state. The current `SettingsPane` exposes Whisper-specific controls despite other selected engines; the redesigned picker must bind to the real selected engine while preserving access to existing advanced settings. The dot calendar may remain, but expose actual dates/minutes through a readable native label or tooltip rather than making a user infer a day from circles.

Approval becomes unconditional after these requirements are represented in the handoff and verified on a native build. Website work and the forthcoming welcome journey are separate reviews.

## Welcome journey senior review

**Spec verdict: APPROVE WITH REQUIRED FIXES.** The five-chapter bridge from the existing warm app to the new instrument is coherent. User-controlled progression, no implicit downloads/engine changes, campaign-based acknowledgment, first-install exclusion and static Reduced Motion states are the right defaults. This is new welcome behavior and should be implemented independently of faithful screen restyling.

1. Correct chapter 3’s visual/caption: postprocessing yields Speaker 1/2 for unknown voices, with an explicit rename affordance; names appear automatically only for a recognized stored voice. Keep sample labeling visible.
2. Auto-present only on the initial active, idle main-window launch before editing. If launch is busy, retain the pending campaign and offer a quiet “What’s new” entry after work settles, or wait for the next idle launch; never open a delayed modal over a user mid-session. Defer manual replay during active work, restore focus after dismissal, and never cover setup/error recovery. Dismissal acknowledgment applies to a journey actually presented, not merely a parent window closing. The parent has selected this policy, satisfying the interruption concern at design level.
3. At 560×480, allow chapter content to scroll with the chapter rail and navigation pinned; do not shrink explanatory type to satisfy a no-scrolling promise. Keep the English-only qualification in the same reading region as the BAPS claim. Verify the dark counterpart. The initial HTML forces light and stacks the compact layout without pinning controls; its first-scene morph is also triggered only after leaving that scene. Run the transformation while its scene is visible through an explicit user action or once after entry, and use its completed state with Reduced Motion. These visual details must match the spec before approval is unconditional.
4. Keep the launch-eligibility rule concrete before implementation: a migration-stable prior-install signal must distinguish genuinely new installs from migrated installs without meetings. An unresolved “preferences and/or history” heuristic is not yet sufficient for the once-only acceptance criterion. If reliable history is unavailable, offer replay/opt-in rather than misclassifying fresh installs.

## Cinematic revision direction

The user now explicitly prefers stronger spatial movement and dramatic transitions. Earlier approval established usability and visual consistency; it does not settle this newer immersion goal. Keep monochrome and everyday working efficiency while revising welcome composition and transition choreography.

- Replace nested stage cards with one shared scene filling the welcome body. One waveform/meeting artifact persists as it becomes transcript rows, structured notes and a local library; do not reset the composition into five unrelated slides.
- Use a decisive 700–1000ms reframe on chapter activation: pull back, travel through a workspace, then settle on the next useful object. Foreground instrument and background plane may move at different depths. Scale and position should explain a relationship, not decorate an unchanged card.
- Keep controls pinned and copy readable in a stable region. The user advances chapters; no timed copy loss or mandatory playback. Reduced Motion goes directly to the same complete end compositions, without depth travel.
- Give the ending a spatial destination: the library/workspace becomes the app context rather than another promotional card. Preserve illustrative labeling and honest provider/model/speaker claims.
- Keep ordinary app navigation immediate and familiar. Cinematic treatment belongs to welcome and a small number of meaningful state transitions, with no recurring delay on everyday actions.

The website should follow the same continuity principle: a broad journey environment and recognizable persistent meeting artifact, rather than a small framed scene beside large empty chapter spacing. Stronger movement is approved when it clarifies the narrative; additional simultaneous loops, floating cards and decorative glows are not the goal. Revised prototype and native evidence pending.
